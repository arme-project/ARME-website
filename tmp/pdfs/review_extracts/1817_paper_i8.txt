Figure 7: Objective-performance summary using Normalised RT
(blue) and MT(red). The control condition anchors the continuous
fit at 0 cm. Baseline is excluded so that the zero-offset case is not
overrepresented. The curves show the fitted quadratic mixed-effects

For display clarity, the y-axis is clipped at 2.2, and the number

clipped values were retained in all statistical analyses and model fits.
Across both views, temporal cost increases with misalignment mag-
nitude while the zero-offset conditions remain close to Control.
B.2.2 Details on Statistical Analysis
Appendix Table 5 reports the planned contrasts from the expanded
categorical mixed-effects models used for Figure 3. Ratios are re-
ported on the control-normalised scale, so values above 1.0 indi-
cate slower performance than the corresponding Control reference.
The categorical analysis shows that Baseline was slightly faster than
Control aligned for both RT and MT, that RT was elevated at every
misaligned offset relative to Control offset, and that MT diverged
reliably from Control offset from the Half condition onward.

B.3 Correctness
Appendix Figure 8 summarizes mean error rate during the offset
phase across conditions. Baseline and Control remained at or near
0% error, indicating that participants were highly accurate when
no spatial misalignment was present. Error remained low in the
Small condition (1.0%) but increased sharply by Half (8.3%) and
continued to rise through Medium (15.0%), Large (16.7%), VLarge
(17.1%), and Huge (18.9%). Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on
participant-level offset-phase error rates, compared against Control
and Holm-corrected across the six misaligned conditions, showed
a small but reliable increase in the Small condition (W=0,p=
.046) and larger increases from Half onward (all correctedp< .001;
Appendix Table 7). This pattern is consistent with the RT and MT
analyses: larger registration errors not only slowed performance but
also reduced response accuracy, with the clearest accuracy costs
emerging from the medium-to-large offsets onward.
Figure 8: Supplementary mean error rate during the offset phase
by condition. Bars show the participant mean and error bars indicate
SEM across participants. Baseline and Control remained near 0% er-
ror, whereas error increased with offset magnitude, from 1.0% in the
Small condition to 18.9% in the Huge condition. Formal participant-
level contrasts against Control are reported in Appendix Table 7.
Appendix Table 7 reports supplementary correctness statistics
corresponding to Appendix Figure 8. Error rates are summarized
as participant-level offset-phase percentages, and inferential com-
parisons use paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests against the Control
condition with Holm correction across the six misaligned condi-
tions. These tests show that error remained at floor for Baseline
and Control, increased slightly but reliably in the Small condition,
and rose substantially from Half onward.

B.4 Learning/Fatigue Analyses
Figure 9: Supplementary descriptive summary of learning/fatigue
across the six randomized switch-condition blocks. Transparent
points show participant means for aligned and offset phases, and
black markers indicate the group mean±SEM. Baseline and Con-
trol were excluded because their positions were fixed at the start and
end of the session. The figure is consistent with the inferential anal-
ysis: normalized RT remains broadly stable across switch-block or-
der, normalized MT shows a modest reduction in the offset phase
over later blocks, and correctness does not exhibit a reliable across-
session decline.
