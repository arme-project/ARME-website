AR-COMPQ: A Dual-Path Perceptual Quality Assessment for Augmented
Reality Images Composition
Figure 1: Snapshot of the AR composition data construction and quality assessment model operation.
ABSTRACT
Augmented Reality (AR) applications depend on the seamless com-
position of virtual objects within real-world environments. In prac-
tice, however, imperfections in sensing, rendering, and composit-
ing pipelines introduce distortions that degrade the perceived re-
alism of AR imagery. The perceptual impact of these distortions
is highly content-dependent and varies across different distortion
types, making reliable quality assessment of AR image composi-
tion particularly challenging. Existing image quality assessment
(IQA) metrics, primarily designed for natural images, do not ade-
quately capture the perceptual characteristics of distortions arising
in AR compositions.
In this work, we investigate perceptual quality in AR image com-
position through a unified framework that combines dataset con-
struction, perceptual analysis, and quality modeling. We first in-
troduce a new AR composition quality dataset containing diverse
scene contexts with systematically controlled distortions affecting
both the structural properties of virtual objects (geometry and tex-
ture) and appearance consistency (brightness, color, and contrast).
Using this dataset, we conduct a subjective evaluation to analyze
human sensitivity to different distortion types and to obtain percep-
tual quality annotations.
Based on the insights from this perceptual analysis, we propose
an objective AR composition quality assessment model that explic-
itly accounts for both structural distortions impacting object fidelity
and appearance distortions affecting photometric harmony between
virtual objects and the surrounding scene. Experimental results
show that the proposed approach better predicts human perceptual
judgments of AR composition quality than existing IQA methods
across diverse content and distortion conditions.
Index Terms:Augmented Reality, Image Composition, Image
Quality, Quality of Experience, Visual Perception.
1 INTRODUCTION
Augmented Reality (AR) systems aim to seamlessly integrate vir-
tual objects into the physical world by compositing rendered con-
tent into camera-captured scenes. Achieving perceptually convinc-
ing image compositions is fundamental for many AR applications,
including interactive retail visualization, remote assistance, immer-
sive education, navigation, and product visualization. As AR tech-
nologies rapidly transition from research prototypes to widely de-
ployed consumer platforms on smartphones, wearable devices, and
lightweight head-mounted displays, AR has become an increas-
ingly important medium for delivering interactive visual experi-
ences [22]. In such scenarios, users continuously evaluate the re-
alism of the augmented content, and even minor visual inconsis-
tencies can disrupt the illusion of integration and negatively affect
the overall Quality-of-Experience (QoE). In consumer-facing ap-
plications such as online shopping or interior design visualization,
the perceived realism of virtual objects can directly influence user
trust, engagement, and decision-making [3]. Consequently, ensur-
ing high perceptual quality in AR image composition has become
an important challenge for modern AR systems.
Despite substantial progress in real-time rendering, tracking, and
scene understanding, AR image composition often introduces ar-
tifacts that degrade perceptual realism. These artifacts may arise
from limitations in sensing accuracy, rendering pipelines, and de-
vice resources, as well as mismatches between virtual objects and
the surrounding environment. Practical constraints such as limited
computational resources, bandwidth restrictions, and aggressive
compression—particularly on mobile and wearable platforms—can

in AR image compositions can be broadly categorized into two
groups. The first group consists of structural distortions, which
affect the structural fidelity of the virtual object itself. Examples
include geometric distortions such as shape deformation or spa-
tial misalignment, and texture degradations, such as blurred or cor-
rupted surface details. The second group includes appearance dis-
tortions, which influence the photometric consistency between the
virtual object and the real scene, including brightness inconsisten-
cies, color shifts, contrast mismatches, and blending artifacts. Ad-
ditional compositional artifacts may also arise from inaccurate oc-
clusion handling, boundary inconsistencies, or shadow mismatches
introduced during AR rendering pipelines [2]. The perceptual im-
pact of these distortions is often highly dependent on scene context,
background complexity, and object characteristics, making their in-
fluence on perceived AR quality difficult to quantify.
Reliable quality assessment methods are therefore essential for
analyzing AR composition pipelines and guiding perceptually op-
timized system design. Image Quality Assessment (IQA) has been
extensively studied for natural images and videos, with widely used
metrics such as PSNR, SSIM [24], FSIM [30], and VIF [19]. More
recent deep perceptual metrics, including LPIPS [31], DISTS [5],
and DreamSim [8], have demonstrated improved correlation with
human judgments on natural-image datasets. However, these ap-
proaches are primarily designed for conventional image distortions
and rely on pixel-level fidelity or learned natural-image statistics.
Consequently, they are not well suited to capture the perceptual
characteristics of AR image composition, where perceived realism
depends not only on object quality but also on the contextual rela-
tionship between virtual objects and real-world environments. In
particular, AR imagery often involves distortions related to spatial
alignment, appearance harmony, and object–background consis-
tency that are not adequately represented in traditional IQA frame-
works [23, 29].
Only a limited number of studies have investigated perceptual
phenomena specific to AR imagery. For instance, Duanet al.ex-
plored perceptual ambiguity in AR compositions and introduced
datasets and metrics for analyzing visual confusion in augmented
scenes [6, 7]. Other work has examined the perceptual impact of il-
lumination inconsistencies between virtual objects and real scenes,
demonstrating that lighting mismatches can significantly degrade
perceived realism [12]. More recently, several AR-oriented quality
assessment models have been proposed to address the limitations
of traditional IQA methods, including blind AR image quality as-
sessment frameworks and lightweight transformer-based predictors
designed for AR imagery [15, 17, 18]. While these efforts provide
valuable insights into AR perception, existing studies typically fo-
cus on limited distortion types, restricted compositing scenarios, or
relatively small datasets. A systematic understanding of how differ-
ent distortions influence perceived AR composition quality across
diverse scene content remains largely unexplored, and current ob-
jective metrics still struggle to reliably predict human perceptual
judgments under such conditions.
In this paper, we address these challenges through a comprehen-
sive investigation of perceptual quality in AR image composition.
We first introduce a new AR image composition quality dataset that
contains diverse scene contexts with systematically controlled dis-
tortions affecting both structural properties of virtual objects (ge-
ometry and texture) and appearance consistency (brightness, color,
and contrast). Using this dataset, we conduct a large-scale subjec-
tive evaluation to obtain Mean Opinion Scores (MOS) and analyze
human sensitivity to different distortion types across varying scene
conditions. Building upon insights from this perceptual analysis,
we further develop an objective AR composition quality assessment
model designed to capture both structural distortions that affect ob-
ject fidelity and appearance distortions that influence photometric
harmony between virtual objects and real environments. Exper-
imental results demonstrate that the proposed approach provides
improved prediction of perceptual quality compared with existing
IQA metrics.
The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
• AR Composition Quality Dataset: We introduce a new dataset
for AR image composition quality assessment containing di-
verse scenes with controlled structural (geometry and texture)
and appearance (brightness, color, and contrast) distortions.
• Perceptual Study and Analysis: We conduct a comprehensive
subjective study to analyze human sensitivity to different dis-
tortion types and content variations, providing perceptual in-
sights into AR composition quality.
• Objective AR Quality Assessment Model: We propose a
distortion-aware quality assessment model that jointly con-
siders structural fidelity and appearance consistency, achiev-
ing improved prediction performance compared with existing
IQA metrics.
2 SUBJECTIVEANALYSIS OFAR QUALITY
Despite the rapid deployment of AR, efforts toward systematic data
generation and benchmarking for AR image quality assessment re-
main limited. Existing studies either focus on simplified compo-
sition models or consider only a narrow range of perceptual fac-
tors [6, 7, 12]. For instance Duan et al. [6, 7] investigated alpha
blending composition in AR content with just limited effort to add
compression factor. Realistic object rendering and structural align-
ment, which are important in applications such as AR shopping, are
not considered in these studies. Guti´errezet al.[12] examined illu-
mination variations but their dataset was restricted to four contents
and did not capture the broader diversity of AR rendering artifacts.

To address these limitations, we introduce a comprehensive
benchmark for AR composition image quality assessment, named
ARC-IQA, and conduct a controlled subjective study to obtain re-
liable perceptual ground truth. The dataset is designed to reflect re-
alistic AR usage conditions by incorporating diverse object types,
backgrounds, and distortion sources relevant to mobile AR applica-
tions.
2.1 ARC-IQA Dataset
2.1.1 AR Content Selection
Virtual objects used in ARC-IQA are selected from the Ama-
zon–Berkeley 3D Object Dataset [9], which provides high-
resolution, high-quality 3D models of everyday objects. These
models exhibit diverse material properties, textures, and geometric
complexity, making them well suited for perceptual quality anal-
ysis. From this dataset, seven representative objects are selected
to span a wide range of color distributions, textural richness, and
shape complexity.
For each object, a corresponding real background scene is care-
fully chosen and captured using a mobile device to ensure clear
visibility, appropriate scale, and realistic placement of the virtual
object. This setup enables controlled evaluation of object realism
while maintaining natural scene context.
2.1.2 Distortion Design
In the generation of the dataset, the pristine selected contents are
used to apply some major distortions observed in practical AR sys-
tems particularly on 3D object features such as mesh and texture
and then distorted object is blended with corresponding background
in an AR device platform (Fig. 1). A snapshot image of each com-
position is used for AR quality assessment. We discuss the main
distortion types applied in ARC-IQA dataset. Fig. 3 shows sample
snapshots of content with different distortions applied in AR com-
position.
Object-level distortionsare introduced directly on the 3D
model of virtual object prior to any composition. We applied two
object-level distortions, geometric distortion and texture distortion.
Geometric distortions are generated by applying random vertex dis-
placements to the object mesh simulating inaccuracies and defor-
mation artifacts. Considering the original mesh defined by a set of
verticesV={v i ∈R 3}N

Texture distortions are applied by performing lossy compression
on the object’s texture maps, introducing blur and blocking artifacts
that commonly arise in real-time rendering pipelines.
Appearance-level distortionssimulate photometric inconsis-
tencies between the rendered object and the real scene. We ma-
nipulate three attributes: color balance, contrast, and brightness.
For each attribute, distorted versions are generated by progressively
increasing or decreasing the parameter across three severity levels
relative to the reference setting.
Composition-level distortionsmodel errors arising during AR
scene integration. These include incorrect object positioning, oc-
clusion mismatches between virtual and real elements, and blend-
ing artifacts at object boundaries. Four representative composition
noise patterns are applied to sample content to capture these effects.
2.1.3 Mobile AR Capture Pipeline
We develop a custom ARCore-based mobile application to place
and render virtual 3D objects within real scenes while controlling
distortion parameters and rendering settings. For each distortion
configuration, a 2D snapshot of the composed AR scene is captured,
producing paired reference and distorted images for image-based
quality assessment.
The resulting dataset, as summarize in Tab. 1, termed ARC-IQA,
comprises diverse AR compositions spanning multiple contents,
distortion types, and severity levels. The selected parameters for
distortions are chosen to bring some noticeable change and at same
time tried to cover wide range of distortion levels. Representative
dataset examples are shown in Fig. 3.
2.2 Subjective Study
We conducted a subjective study with 15 participants (ages 21–45
years) screened for normal vision; stimuli were shown in random-
ized order under controlled lighting at native resolution with a 2-ft
viewing distance; sessions lasted approximately 30 minutes to pre-
vent fatigue and included a standardized training phase with inde-
pendent anchor content.
Using the generated ARC-IQA dataset, we conduct a subjective
quality assessment study to obtain perceptual ground-truth annota-
tions. In total, 193 AR image snapshots are selected, covering all
contents and distortion categories.
All experiments have been conducted in accordance with
the ITU-T P.910 recommendation [1] for subjective multime-
dia quality assessment. A Double Stimulus Continuous Quality
Scale (DSCQS) methodology is adopted, in which participants are
shown the reference and distorted AR images side by side and asked
to rate the perceived quality of the distorted image on a continuous
scale ranging from 0 to 100. We had 15 participants in total with
age 21 to 45 years old. The stimuli were presented in random or-
der to each participant using different random seeds. Participants
were seated at a fixed viewing distance of approximately two feet
in a light-controlled laboratory environment. Each session started
with a training session with different contents than original dataset
and total session lasted∼30 mins. To ensure consistency across

3 PROPOSEDMETHOD
3.1 Overview
The proposed framework decomposes AR perceptual realism as-
sessment into two complementary dimensions:object-level fidelity
andappearance-level harmony. The former evaluates structural
consistency between distorted and pristine compositions, while the
latter measures cross-domain appearance discrepancy relative to a
perceptually harmonized prior.
As illustrated in Fig. 4, the model consists of: (1) a shared back-
bone encoder, (2) a fidelity assessment branch, (3) a harmony as-
sessment branch, and (4) a lightweight regression head for quality
prediction.
3.2 Shared Feature Encoding
First, a harmonization module is applied to the distorted imageI D
to generate harmonized imageI H. Given distorted compositionI D,
pristine referenceI R, and its harmonized reconstructionI H, all in-
puts are processed by a shared backbone encoderφ(·):

whereφ:R H×W×3 →R C×h×w maps images to deep feature rep-
resentations. Weight sharing ensures that all representations lie in a
common embedding manifold, facilitating meaningful cross-image
comparisons.

4 EXPERIMENTS
4.1 Experimental Setup
The proposed AR-COMPQ, is trained and evaluated on the ARC-
IQA dataset. Cross-validation on other dataset (e.g. CFIQA [7]
and ARIQA [6]) was not appropriate as there is a significant shift
of target on them with focusing on blending and confusion of in-
formation. To ensure content-independent evaluation and provide
stronger generalization evaluation, a leave-one-content-out cross-
validation strategy is adopted. Specifically, in each split, all samples
corresponding to one AR content are reserved for testing, while the
remaining contents are used for training. This procedure is repeated
exhaustively so that each content appears once as the test set, and
the reported results are obtained by averaging across all splits.
For the base feature extractor in the proposed method, we se-
lected SqueezeNet pre-trained on the ImageNet dataset. To pre-
serve generic visual representations and reduce overfitting given the
dataset size, the weights of the base network are kept frozen, and
only the task-specific layers of AR-COMPQ are trained.
The distorted image and corresponding pristine version on ARC-
IQA are used as main inputs to network along with a harmonized
version synthesized using the AICT harmonization model [16]
which is optimized for providing efficient high resolution image
harmonization. The MOS values from the subjective study are used
as ground-truth quality labels.

4.2 Quantitative Results
Tab. 2 reports correlation-based (PLCC, SRCC, KRCC) and error-
based (RMSE, MAE) performance of representative IQA metrics
on the ARC-IQA dataset. We applied monotonic nonlinear fitting
of scores for traditional method by following similar approach in
using logistic function in [7]. Overall, the proposed AR-COMPQ
consistently outperforms all other methods, with the highest cor-
relations and the lowest prediction errors. In contrast, conventional
pixel-based, structural, and perceptual metrics exhibit limited align-
ment with subjective AR quality, while deep feature-based metrics
show strong inverse correlations. These results demonstrate that
AR-COMPQ more accurately captures perceptual factors specific
to AR content, leading to superior agreement with human judg-
ments.

4.3 Perceptual Sensitivity Analysis Across Content and
Distortions
To further understand the behavior and robustness of the proposed
AR-COMPQ model, we analyze its prediction performance across
different scene contents, distortion categories, and distortion sever-
ity levels. This analysis provides insight into how well the model
captures perceptual variations that arise from content-dependent
distortions in AR image composition.
4.3.1 Performance Across Scene Contents
Fig. 5 illustrates the prediction performance of AR-COMPQ across
seven different AR scene contents in the ARC-IQA dataset. Each
content corresponds to a unique background environment and vir-
tual object configuration, resulting in varying visual complexity and
compositional difficulty.
The results show that the proposed model maintains consis-
tently high prediction accuracy across most contents. However,
moderate variations can be observed for scenes with highly tex-
tured backgrounds or extreme lighting conditions. For example
the office chair scene contains low lighting condition with uniform
texture which is difficult for user to distinguish distortions while
our method usually under estimate quality score. The complex-
ity of background in 3baskets and sofa pich is also resulting in
lower performance on them. Such example scenes typically intro-
duce stronger contextual interactions between the virtual object and
the surrounding environment, making perceptual quality prediction
more challenging. Nevertheless, AR-COMPQ demonstrates stable
performance across contents, indicating that the model generalizes
well to diverse AR scenarios.
4.3.2 Performance Across Distortion T ypes
We further analyze the prediction behavior across different distor-
tion categories, including appearance distortions (brightness, color,
and contrast) and structural distortions (texture and geometry).
Fig. 6 presents the prediction results grouped by distortion type.
The results reveal that AR-COMPQ maintains strong agreement
with subjective scores across all distortion categories. Bright-
ness and Contrast distortions generally produce smoother predic-
tion trends due to their global photometric effects, whereas struc-
tural distortions introduce more localized visual artifacts that can
lead to larger perceptual variations. Also, color harmony predic-
tions show larger variation as the perception of color could be com-
plex and more background dependent. Despite these differences,
the proposed model effectively captures both structural and appear-
ance degradations, demonstrating the benefit of explicitly modeling
these two complementary distortion dimensions in the proposed ar-
chitecture as two complementary paths.
4.3.3 Joint Analysis of Performance on Distortion and Con-
tent
Figure 7 presents the prediction error of AR-COMPQ across differ-
ent AR contents and distortion types, measured using Mean Abso-
lute Error (MAE) between predicted quality scores and the ground-
truth ratings. Overall, the model shows consistently low errors
across most contents, indicating stable performance across diverse
AR scenes. Some variations can be observed depending on scene
characteristics; for example, contents such as bookshelf exhibit uni-
formly low errors, while scenes with more complex structures, such
as 3baskets and office chair, show slightly higher errors for certain
distortions.
Across distortion categories, prediction errors remain rela-
tively balanced, suggesting that the proposed model generalizes
well across different distortion conditions. While certain con-
tent–distortion combinations produce higher deviations, the over-
all error distribution remains bounded, demonstrating that AR-
COMPQ maintains reliable quality prediction across varying scene
contents and distortion scenarios.

contrast). Figure 8 shows the relationship between predicted qual-

5 ABLATIONSTUDY
To systematically investigate the contribution of individual archi-
tectural components and design choices within the proposed AR-
COMPQ framework, we conducted an extensive ablation study.
The analysis focuses on three key aspects: (i) the configuration of
input paths, (ii) the influence of backbone architecture and model
capacity, All ablation experiments are performed on the ARC-IQA
dataset under identical training settings to ensure fair comparisons.

configurations: reference–distorted, reference–harmonized, and
distorted–harmonized. All models are trained and evaluated under
identical settings to isolate the effect of input selection.
The quantitative results are summarized in Table 3. Among the
evaluated configurations, the ”reference,distorted,harmonized vari-

6 CONCLUSION
In this paper, we presented a comprehensive investigation of per-
ceptual quality in augmented reality (AR) image composition. We
introduced theARC-IQAdataset, which systematically incorpo-
rates object-level, appearance-level, and composition-level distor-
tions generated through a realistic AR rendering pipeline. A con-
trolled subjective study was conducted to analyze human sensitivity
to these distortions across diverse scene contents.
Guided by this perceptual analysis, we proposedAR-COMPQ,
a dual-path objective quality assessment model that jointly captures
object-level structural fidelity and appearance-level harmony be-
tween virtual objects and real scenes. Experimental results demon-
strate that AR-COMPQ achieves stronger agreement with human
perceptual judgments compared to conventional IQA metrics and
recent deep perceptual models.
Overall, this work provides a new benchmark and a perceptually
grounded framework for evaluating AR composition quality, sup-
porting future research in perceptually optimized AR systems.
Future work will explore view-dependent and dynamic AR sce-
narios enabled by head-mounted displays and extend the dataset to
include more complex compositions, potentially leveraging gener-
ative AI for scalable data synthesis.
